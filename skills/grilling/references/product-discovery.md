# Product and task discovery

Read for an explicit product/idea interview or when the current task cannot be
understood without resolving several dependent product decisions. Start from
the user's actual context and existing documents, not an empty questionnaire.

## Build the decision tree

Identify the intended result, who uses it and what remains unknown. Keep a
compact set of settled decisions, ready questions and dependent unknowns in
the existing task record or working context. A separate tracker is unnecessary.

Explore the applicable branches, following dependencies rather than this order:

- User and process: who acts, what triggers the work, what they do today, where
  the proposed product fits and which part is in scope.
- Outcome and value: what becomes possible, the current alternative, why the
  change matters and how the owner would recognize a useful result. Owner
  beliefs about demand remain hypotheses until supported by customer evidence.
- Concrete path: a representative input, the actions and state changes, the
  resulting output and who observes it. Ask for a real example when broad words
  hide different behaviors.
- Rules and ownership: business terms, roles, permissions, authoritative data,
  decision ownership and what a human must resolve.
- Exceptions: duplicate or partial work, correction, cancellation, unavailable
  integrations and disputed outcomes, when applicable to the actual process.
- Constraints and acceptance: consequential limits on data, workload, cost or
  release, the first useful slice and the scenario that establishes readiness.

Do not turn these branches into a universal form. Answer repository facts by
inspection. Ask the owner for intent and rules that sources cannot establish.
Choose technical mechanisms yourself under the project's architecture.

## Follow the answer

After each round, state what was settled when useful, revise the tree and ask
the next ready questions. If an answer changes the premise, revisit affected
branches rather than continuing the old script. Reuse answers from earlier
messages and documents; do not repeatedly request the same business decision.

Example: "a customer submits a request" leaves multiple meanings. Establish
whether it is an inquiry or a committed order before asking about its approval
and payment transitions. Once the owner chooses inquiry, ask the applicable
follow-ups about assignment and repeated contact; do not silently assume the
same transitions as a paid order. Examples probe intent, not actual user facts.

Short rounds may continue for as long as consequential dependencies require.
Let the user pause or change focus. If they explicitly ask for no questions,
preserve unknowns and reversible assumptions with their status under host policy.

## Close on usable understanding

Finish when the requested area has a coherent user/process, result, important
rules and limits, and a concrete acceptance scenario. Show remaining unknowns,
which work they block and which can be investigated later. Do not claim that
every possible future question is exhausted.

Persist accepted decisions and reasons with the current requirements/task
owner. Use domain-modeling for terms or durable architecture decisions when
needed; avoid duplicate specifications. Return to Project Start for foundation
or Task Delivery for implementation within the original authorization. An
interview-only request ends with the understanding and open items.
