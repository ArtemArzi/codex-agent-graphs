# Internal links, external presence and local search

Select the requested branch. Read the site's URL inventory, content owners and
known redirects first. Use current primary guidance linked in
[sources.md](sources.md) when provider rules affect a decision.

## Internal linking

Combine known published URLs with an observed link crawl or project-generated
link graph. A crawl alone cannot reveal every orphan: compare its reachable set
with the route/content inventory and available search-console URLs. Report the
coverage and limits of any sample. No arbitrary depth or link-count threshold
establishes quality for all sites.

For each important page, identify a useful path from another relevant page.
Inspect menus, breadcrumbs and contextual links; meaningful `<a href>` links
should lead to the intended working destination. A sitemap entry is not an
internal navigation link. Follow actual status/redirect/canonical behaviour;
replace stale links with the intended final URL where appropriate. Compare source
and rendered links when scripts produce navigation.

Choose anchor text that describes the destination naturally. A service article
can link to the relevant service and real branch; a branch page can link to
available services and booking. These are patterns, not permission to invent
services or create every combination of branch and keyword. Avoid repeated
keyword anchors, unrelated link blocks and doorway pages. Keep external source
citations useful rather than applying `nofollow` to all outgoing links.

Deliver concrete source-page → destination → proposed placement/text changes,
the observed reason and checks. When implementing, verify both the link in the
published artifact and its destination; include no-JS checks where the project
requires them. Keep content approval and release authority with existing owners.

## Incoming links and brand mentions

Use available backlink reports, Search Console evidence and actual referring
pages. A search snippet alone does not confirm a working link, its attributes,
audience or current placement. Record observation date and tool coverage.
Separate incoming links from unlinked brand mentions and outgoing references.
Without a data source, backlink totals, proprietary authority scores, loss rates
and competitor traffic remain unknown; do not reconstruct them from guesses.

For an opportunity, inspect topical/local relevance, editorial quality, real
audience evidence, destination, anchor, placement attributes, durability and cost
when supplied. Vendor "authority" is one tool's metric, not Google's ranking
score. Observed correlation does not prove a placement will improve rankings or
AI citations. Do not create a disavow list from a third-party toxicity score alone.

## Paid placements

Distinguish advertising/sponsorship with a useful audience from buying links to
manipulate rankings. Google's spam policy covers the latter; paid advertising
links should be qualified with `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow`. Check the actual
publication and link, not just the seller's promise. Other engines and platforms
have their own current rules.

A proposal can compare audiences, prices, referral/conversion measurement and
terms if the user supplies them. Leave missing price, budget and effectiveness
unknown. Do not promise link equity, indexing, permanent placement or citations.
Purchases, outreach messages and publication require authority for those actions;
research alone grants none. Use existing campaign/measurement owners if present,
without installing tools or imposing a separate campaign workflow.

## Local business presence

For a business serving real places, reconcile the confirmed business/branch
facts with its website and the directories/maps actually used in that market.
Check name, address, phone, hours, service differences, booking URL and duplicate
or obsolete profiles. Record disagreements and the factual owner before editing.
Read profiles publicly when possible; account verification and profile mutations
are separate authorised work.

Use truthful page-specific LocalBusiness subtypes and visible facts; structured
data is not a directory submission. Assess local query/region needs from actual
business geography. Do not fabricate locations, reviews or region pages. A
directory listing, organic ranking, map result and AI citation remain different
outcomes; AI-answer comparisons belong to AI Search Visibility.
